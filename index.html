The problem with every plan you've tried

Most programs are built for someone with nothing else on

Six meals a day. Ninety minutes under the bar. No dinners out, no red-eyes, no season where the business eats the week. It works right up until your real life shows up — and then it's you who feels like the failure.

What a standard plan assumes

  • You can train 5–6 days, an hour-plus, at the same time each day
  • Every meal is cooked at home and weighed
  • Travel weeks and work dinners are "off plan" — start again Monday
  • Progress is measured by the scale alone
  • Questions get answered by a chatbot or a Monday form

How Hybrid Life is built

  • Sessions sized to the day you're having — 40 minutes counts
  • A restaurant and travel playbook written before you need it
  • Calories that cycle with your week instead of ignoring it
  • Body composition, energy, lifts, sleep and mindset — not one number
  • You text your coach. Your coach texts back.

The Hybrid Life principle

The proof is in the schedule

One ordinary Tuesday, start to finish

Not a highlight reel. A real Tuesday, including the meeting that runs over and the hour after the kids go down. If a plan can't survive those two things, it can't survive your life.

05:15

Alarm, then water before coffee

Sixteen ounces with electrolytes before anything else. You're mildly dehydrated every morning and it reads as tiredness, so we fix that before you diagnose yourself as unmotivated.

05:40

Lift — 48 minutes

Push day. Every session in your app has a full version and a 25-minute version. You pick based on the day you're actually having, not the day you planned on Sunday.

06:35

Shower, shake, out

Forty grams of protein before you've made a single decision. Breakfast was settled on Sunday so your first willpower spend of the day isn't on food.

07:20

School run and commute

Nothing to manage here. This is the point — the plan doesn't ask for anything during the part of your morning that belongs to other people.

08:30

Back-to-back calls

Second coffee, and it's the last one. Caffeine after noon costs you sleep, sleep costs you recovery, and recovery is what the training is actually for.

10:45

The gap between meetings

Protein snack from the desk drawer, and a lap of the building. Steps aren't a workout, they're the difference between a 2,400 and 2,700 calorie day without touching your food.

12:30

Client lunch, steakhouse

You order the ribeye and skip the bread basket. The restaurant guide covers this exact situation, so you're not doing arithmetic at the table in front of a prospect.

14:10

The wall

It doesn't arrive today, because your carbs were placed around it rather than eaten at breakfast. Energy is a programming variable, not a personality trait.

16:15

The meeting runs long

Your planned walk disappears. This is the moment most plans break — and the reason yours has a rule for it: you don't make it up, you don't compensate at dinner, you just carry on. One missed walk is noise, not failure.

18:30

Dinner with the family

Same meal as everyone at the table, portioned for you. Nobody eats grilled chicken out of a container while the rest of the house has something else.

20:00

Kids down, kitchen quiet

The genuinely hard hour. There's a planned snack that fits your numbers waiting for exactly this, because willpower at 8pm on a Tuesday is not a strategy.

21:30

Check-in submitted

Weight, steps, sleep, training, and one line on how the week feels. Two minutes. You wake up to adjustments rather than silence.

22:20

Lights out

Seven and a half hours protected on purpose. Sleep is the cheapest performance input you have and the first one everybody trades away.

How the program runs

Four phases, in order

Nobody starts at the hardest setting. Each phase earns the next one, so the plan gets more demanding only as your life proves it can hold it.

Phase 01

Audit

Weeks 1–2

Before anything changes, we find out what's actually happening. Most people aren't failing their plan — they're on the wrong one for their real energy expenditure.

  • Full TDEE and intake audit against your actual week
  • Baseline photos, measurements, lifts, sleep and stress
  • Your calendar mapped — travel, dinners, busy seasons
Phase 02

Build the system

Weeks 3–8

The structure goes in. This phase is about making the plan survive a bad week, not about maximum aggression.

  • Custom nutrition document and training block built around your schedule
  • Restaurant, travel and weekend playbooks written before you need them
  • Weekly check-ins start; we adjust off data, not feel
Phase 03

Push

Weeks 9–16

Now we go after the number you've been stuck at. Calories cycle with your week, training intensity climbs, and the plateau breaks.

  • Carb cycling and day-type structure matched to training load
  • Progressive overload with a short version of every session
  • Mindset work on the decisions that used to derail you
Phase 04

Make it permanent

Week 17 onward

The phase most programs skip, and the reason most results don't last. You leave able to run this yourself.

  • Calories brought back up deliberately instead of by accident
  • Move from weighing food to portions you can eyeball anywhere
  • You learn the why behind every adjustment, so you can make them alone

Seven years ago, I started posting my fitness journey every single day.

At the time, I had no idea where it would lead. I was bouncing between jobs, trying different businesses, and searching for something I could truly build my life around. The one thing I always came back to was fitness.

But my relationship with fitness wasn't always healthy.

For years, I trained like a bodybuilder and built my entire life around the gym. No balance. No real structure. Eating out made me feel guilty. Missing a workout felt like failure. I could be in great shape physically while mentally feeling completely lost.

There were periods where I was depressed, uncertain about my future, and putting everything I had into becoming better physically because it was one of the few things I felt like I could control.

Eventually, I realized I didn't want fitness to be my entire life. I wanted fitness to make my life better.

That's when I discovered hybrid training and everything started to change.

I learned how to lift, run, eat, travel, go out, build a business, and still make progress — without the constant guilt of feeling like I was doing something wrong.

And after seven years of documenting my own journey, that became the foundation of Hybrid Life Coaching.

Before you apply

The questions everyone asks

I travel constantly. Is this going to work?

It's the reason the program exists. Travel weeks get their own structure — airport and hotel options, a lower-equipment training block, and adjusted targets so a trip is a planned phase rather than a hole in the data.

How does online coaching actually work?

Everything runs through your phone. Training and video demos live in the app, your nutrition plan arrives as a document you can print or keep on screen, check-ins are a short weekly form, and messaging is direct to me. No gym visits, no fixed session times, no time zone problem.

I eat out and travel constantly for work. Does that ruin it?

No — it's planned for. You get a restaurant playbook covering steakhouse, sushi, airport and the place with nothing on the menu, plus a travel structure with hotel and low-equipment training. Work dinners are part of the plan, not a break from it.

How much time do I need in the gym?

Three to four sessions a week, 40–60 minutes. Every session also has a compressed version for days that collapse. Consistency at 40 minutes outperforms perfection at ninety.

Do I have to weigh my food?

At the start, for a short period, so we're working from real numbers instead of guesses. Most clients move to portion-based eating within the first phase once the calibration is done.

What does it cost?

Pricing depends on the length of the engagement and is covered on the call. Coaching is a considered purchase, and the application exists so neither of us spends time on a bad fit.

What happens after I apply?

You'll answer a short set of questions about your goals, schedule and history. If it looks like a fit, you'll get a link to book a call with me directly. If it isn't, I'll tell you that and point you somewhere better.
